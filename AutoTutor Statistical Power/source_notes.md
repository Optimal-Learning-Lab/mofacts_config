# Source Notes: Statistical power

Source roles satisfy the module requirement for at least one authoritative source, one misconception or learning-difficulty source, and one examples/problems source.

## POW_NCBI_STATPEARLS
- Role: authoritative/examples
- Title: NCBI Bookshelf StatPearls: Type I and Type II Errors and Statistical Power
- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557530/
- Notes used: Defines statistical power in relation to Type II error and gives applied research consequences.

## POW_GREENLAND
- Role: authoritative/misconception
- Title: Greenland et al.: Statistical tests, P values, confidence intervals, and power: a guide to misinterpretations
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4877414/
- Notes used: Defines power as a pre-study probability of rejecting a test hypothesis under a specified alternative and reviews common misinterpretations.

## POW_APS
- Role: misconception/learning difficulty
- Title: Association for Psychological Science: Powerful Myths
- URL: https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/powerful-myths-statistical-power.html
- Notes used: Discusses misconceptions such as power being an all-purpose quality stamp or post-hoc explanation.

## POW_SCRIBBR
- Role: examples/problems
- Title: Scribbr: Statistical Power and Why It Matters
- URL: https://www.scribbr.com/statistics/statistical-power/
- Notes used: Introductory examples of how sample size, effect size, and alpha affect power and how power analysis guides sample size.
