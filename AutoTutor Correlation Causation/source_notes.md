# Source Notes: Correlation vs. causation

Source roles satisfy the module requirement for at least one authoritative source, one misconception or learning-difficulty source, and one examples/problems source.

## CORR_JMP
- Role: authoritative/examples
- Title: JMP: Correlation vs Causation
- URL: https://www.jmp.com/en_us/statistics-knowledge-portal/what-is-correlation/correlation-vs-causation.html
- Notes used: Explains that correlation quantifies association, not causal direction or confounding; contrasts observational correlations with experimental causal evidence.

## CORR_NATURE
- Role: authoritative
- Title: Nature Methods: Association, correlation and causation
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.3587
- Notes used: Concise methodological source: correlation implies association but not causation; causation may require careful design and assumptions.

## CORR_FRONTIERS
- Role: misconception/learning difficulty
- Title: Frontiers in Psychology: Improving the teaching of 'correlation does not equal causation'
- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1645518/pdf
- Notes used: Identifies learning difficulties including both overclaiming causation and overcorrecting to 'correlation is useless/never evidence'.

## CORR_MIT
- Role: examples/problems
- Title: MIT BLOSSOMS: Correlation and Causation
- URL: https://web.mit.edu/blossoms/sites/default/files/video/transcript/Correlation-Causation-Transcript.pdf
- Notes used: Uses spurious correlation, reverse causality, and confounding examples for learner reasoning.
